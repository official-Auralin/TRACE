\begin{abstract}
We present \TRACE{}, a benchmark of temporal causal explanation in fully specified
finite-state systems. Given a total deterministic Mealy machine, a valid observed
trace and a target output at a finite time, a deterministic compiler constructs
a switch-and-light puzzle. The executable semantic constructor is proved in Lean
to preserve every output round under every input intervention, including machines
with loops and outputs that turn off after turning on. A separately proved
terminal projection removes post-target state distinctions from the display
while preserving the scored event under every intervention. Given the recorded positive target event, a participant selects
one inclusion-minimal set of recorded input
values that both guarantees the event when fixed and permits prevention by changes
confined to that set. This contract distinguishes explanatory minimality from
minimum-cardinality optimization and accepts every valid minimal alternative.
We construct a bounded two-player verification game whose winning condition is
exactly the combined causal predicate. Backward evaluation computes its two
branches; an absorbing-sink encoding relates the finite verdict to minimum-even
parity acceptance. Upward closure yields a terminating deletion algorithm for
finding one minimal explanation. Local recurrence certificates support positive
and absence claims. A standalone dataset constructor is optional: external machine,
trace and target records enter the same compilation boundary. The study design
uses 1,024 agent-test items and a separate
64-item shared human--agent panel. A common session service provides the same
network, recording, teaching examples, intervention budget, and answer contract
to both populations. The browser teaches the task through animated examples;
formal transition tables remain an optional reference.
Human ease, comparative agent performance, and generalization remain empirical
questions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and scope}
A known transition system permits an exact distinction between predicting its
behavior and explaining a recorded event. \TRACE{} asks which recorded inputs
jointly provide a guarantee and a means of preventing that event. The complete
rules are public; the task does not require learning a hidden rule from examples.

TempoBench motivates the separation of forward simulation and temporal causal
attribution over deterministic Mealy executions~\cite{holzer2026tempobenchv3}.
Its version-3 fixing and supported-change conditions motivate our explicit
contract. We do not claim reproduction of its released labels, CORP pipeline,
prompts, or scores. Reasoning alignment motivates the task; file-format and
gold-label compatibility are separate claims that we do not make. The dataset
constructor emits machines and recordings independently of the game. The compiler
imports those records without regenerating their machine or selecting another trace.
The intended human--AI comparison shares ARC's interest in comparing populations,
but does not copy its grid tasks, hidden-rule setting, or measure of general
intelligence.

Broader reactive-system cause synthesis uses automata to represent temporal
causes and effects~\cite{finkbeiner2024corp}. This paper makes no equivalence
claim between those cause languages and its finite cell sets.

The reverse-parity review underlying this project separates nearest-removal
trace necessity from strategy-guarantee necessity. Neither is silently
identified with the combined finite predicate developed here. Its
nearest-removal literal-conjunction specialization yields pivotal singletons
under unrestricted Boolean edits. The strategy question instead requires an
arena of alternative system moves and asks about one policy's universal guarantee
together with its existential violating behavior. Our fixed-machine input
interventions are a different, explicitly defined task.

This bounded verification game does not recover an original synthesis
specification or arena. Time runs forward; the algorithm evaluates possible
continuations backward from the designated event. Arbitrary infinite liveness
and policy replacement are outside this release.

\section{Machine and intervention model}
Let
\[
M=(Q,q_0,\Sigma,\{0,1\},\delta,\lambda),\qquad \Sigma=\{0,1\}^{m},
\]
be a total deterministic Mealy machine. Its run on inputs $x_0,\ldots,x_k$
satisfies
\[
q_{t+1}=\delta(q_t,x_t),\qquad o_t=\lambda(q_t,x_t).
\]
Output uses the pre-transition state and current input. Let $h=k+1$,
$D=\{0,\ldots,k\}\times\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$, and let $a$ be the recorded assignment
to $D$. Define $E(x)$ by $o_k(x)=1$ and require $E(a)$.
Every Boolean assignment to $D$ is admissible. The machine and initial state
remain fixed during intervention; outputs and internal states are recomputed.

The input contract imposes no fixed maximum state, channel or horizon count.
Every supplied instance is finite. Machine cycles are allowed because the target
is evaluated after a finite prefix; infinite-horizon strategy solving is not
needed. The initial contract fixes the target to output 1 at step $k$ and admits
all Boolean input assignments. Environmental constraints and richer event
predicates require a separately specified and verified extension.

A cell identifies its recorded value, including zero. The serialized cell ID is
$tm+r$, and the row index of a valuation is $\sum_r x_r2^r$.
These conventions are public in both modalities. Mealy prefix locality ensures
that inputs after $k$ cannot affect this target.

\begin{definition}[Combined finite explanation]
For $C\subseteq D$, define
\[
G(C)\iff \forall x\;(x|_C=a|_C\Rightarrow E(x)),
\qquad
P(C)\iff \exists x\;(x|_{D\setminus C}=a|_{D\setminus C}\land\neg E(x)).
\]
Write $H(C)=G(C)\land P(C)$. An accepted explanation is a set $C$ for which
$H(C)$ holds and no proper subset satisfies $H$.
\end{definition}

Prevention may change only some selected cells. It is not the assertion that
flipping every selected cell defeats the outcome. XOR at $(1,0)$ has the two-cell
combined explanation, although flipping both preserves the target.
For OR at $(1,1)$, either singleton guarantees the output but neither permits
prevention; the pair satisfies both. For AND at $(1,1)$, either singleton permits
prevention but neither guarantees the output; again the pair is required.

There can be multiple accepted answers of different sizes. Minimality refers
to inclusion, not global minimum cardinality. The participant submits one
accepted explanation, not the family or its union. A separate
\texttt{no\_explanation} verdict is correct when no valid set exists.
An empty cell list does not encode that verdict.

\section{A general machine-to-puzzle construction}
Dataset construction, benchmark compilation and participant interaction are
separate components. The optional constructor supplies machine/trace/target
records. The compiler validates the imported machine and complete observed run,
retains the prefix through the target, and creates one common task. Human and
agent interfaces read this task and use a shared session service and grader.
No switch-network recipe is required to import an external machine.

For each time $t\leq k$ and state $q\in Q$, construct a binary decision tree that
reads the $m$ input channels in order. The decision for channel $r$ refers to
the original cell $tm+r$, and its two branches represent that cell's values.
The leaf for valuation $u$ emits $\lambda(q,u)$ and points to state
$\delta(q,u)$ in the next time layer. Traversing a tree is one semantic round,
regardless of the number of switch decisions. Thus outputs can change every
round; an earlier positive output is not an absorbing success state.

All appearances of a given time/channel cell refer to the same input setting and
explanation mark. Routing nodes introduce no new interventions. Identical
subtrees may be merged by a proved equality reduction, while the original full
cell inventory remains available. With an explicit transition table, the full
unreduced forest has $O(h|Q|2^m)$ nodes. This representation is finite, but can
still be large; equality reduction is not a claim of universal compression.

\begin{theorem}[General construction correspondence]\label{thm:compiler}
For every compatible total deterministic Mealy machine, every finite target
$k$, every input word $x$ through that target and every $t\leq k$, the compiled
puzzle emits exactly $o_t(x)$. The original and compiled tasks have the same
guarantee and prevention predicates and exactly the same inclusion-minimal
explanations. These conclusions also hold after identical-branch reduction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induction on the number of input channels shows that a decision tree reaches
exactly the leaf for its supplied valuation. That leaf preserves both the emitted
output and successor state. Induction on rounds then preserves every output and
state transition. Equality reduction preserves tree evaluation because choosing
between equal subtrees changes no result. Cell IDs are injective in time/channel
pairs; the assignment domain and actual values remain unchanged. Replacing the
effect by an extensionally equal effect therefore preserves both quantifiers,
their conjunction and inclusion minimality.
\end{proof}

The compiler CLI calls the executable \texttt{compileRoundReduced} definition
proved in Lean. Structural
recursion establishes termination for each finite tree and replay prefix.
Deterministic channel order, state order and equality reduction fix the semantic
artifact for a given canonical input and compiler version. Canonicalization
under arbitrary renaming or machine isomorphism is not claimed.

The semantic forest retains the complete transition pair at every round,
including the target. The final visual decision tree instead observes only its
output: map each terminal leaf $(\delta(q,u),\lambda(q,u))$ to
$\lambda(q,u)$ and reduce equal branches again. Denote this projection by
$\Pi(T)=\operatorname{reduce}(\operatorname{map}_{\mathrm{snd}}T)$.
All true leaves lead to one shared target lamp; false leaves lead to a distinct
exit that leaves the target dark. Intermediate outputs are status indicators,
visually distinct from the goal. A final-round control can disappear from a
particular route when only the post-target state varies; it can remain relevant
on another route, and its original cell stays in the intervention inventory.

\begin{theorem}[Terminal display correspondence]\label{thm:display}
For every semantic switch tree $T$ and input assignment $x$,
$\operatorname{read}(\Pi(T),x)=\mathrm{snd}(\operatorname{read}(T,x))$.
Following the unchanged semantic transitions before $k$ and the projected tree
at $k$ therefore returns exactly $o_k(x)$ for every $x$. The resulting task has
the same $G$, $P$ and inclusion-minimal explanations as the source machine.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induction on the tree shows that mapping its leaves commutes with evaluation.
Equality reduction preserves that evaluation. Before the target, successor
states are unchanged by Theorem~\ref{thm:compiler}; at the target the projection
returns precisely the transition output. Extensional target equality with the
unchanged input and candidate domains preserves the quantified predicates and
minimality. The semantic forest still preserves the complete state sequence;
the output-only terminal display deliberately does not encode the final state.
\end{proof}

The CLI uses the proved executable \texttt{compileTerminal} definition for
this projection. Applying the same output-only reduction to earlier rounds
would be unsound in general: equal current outputs can lead to different
future outputs. Serialization of the projected tree and its depiction as one
shared visual goal remain separately tested application obligations.

\section{Backward evaluation and the verification game}
Define
\[
\Bad(F)\iff \exists x\;(x|_F=a|_F\land\neg E(x)).
\]
Then $G(C)=\neg\Bad(C)$ and $P(C)=\Bad(D\setminus C)$.
Let $A_F(t)$ contain exactly the input valuations respecting fixed cells in
round $t$. Compute backward:
\[
R_F(t,q)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\bigvee_{u\in A_F(k)}[\lambda(q,u)=0], & t=k,\\[3pt]
\displaystyle\bigvee_{u\in A_F(t)}R_F(t+1,\delta(q,u)), & t<k.
\end{cases}
\]
\begin{theorem}[Replay correspondence]\label{thm:replay}
$R_F(t,q)$ is true exactly when some permitted input suffix from pre-round state
$q$ defeats the target at round $k$. In particular,
$\Bad(F)=R_F(0,q_0)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on remaining rounds. At the target, the recurrence tests exactly the
permitted current-input outputs. Before the target, every permitted suffix
consists of a permitted valuation followed by a permitted suffix from its
deterministic successor. The disjunction and induction hypothesis give both
directions. The terminal test retains the transition output; final state alone
is insufficient.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Two-branch verification game]
Fix $C$ before play. At the root a challenger selects a branch.
In the guarantee branch, the challenger supplies inputs outside $C$,
values in $C$ stay recorded, and the explainer wins iff $E$.
In the prevention branch, the explainer supplies inputs inside $C$,
values outside $C$ stay recorded, and the explainer wins iff $\neg E$.
The machine transitions are forced.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[Game correspondence]
The explainer wins from the root iff $H(C)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Winning the guarantee branch requires success against every permitted completion,
exactly $G(C)$. Winning prevention requires one permitted defeating completion,
exactly $P(C)$. The challenger selects the branch after $C$ is fixed, so both
are required. The candidate is not selected adaptively after counterfactual input.
\end{proof}

Attach a priority-0 absorbing sink after a successful terminal transition and a
priority-1 absorbing sink after failure. Every play reaches its sink in bounded
time. Its least infinitely recurring priority is even iff the terminal verdict
is successful. This is an exact parity encoding of the bounded game; general
infinite parity solving is unnecessary. After the branch choice, only one
player has meaningful choices in each branch.

\section{Minimality and an executable algorithm}
\begin{theorem}[Upward closure and local minimality]\label{thm:min}
Both $G$ and $P$ are upward-closed in $C$. A set is an accepted explanation iff
$H(C)$ and $H(C\setminus\{c\})$ fails for every $c\in C$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Adding fixed cells restricts completions in $G$, preserving a guarantee.
Adding editable cells enlarges possible witnesses in $P$, preserving prevention.
If a valid proper subset $A\subset C$ existed, choose $c\in C\setminus A$;
upward closure would make $C\setminus\{c\}$ valid. The converse follows from
inclusion minimality.
\end{proof}

Start with $C=D$. If $H(D)$ fails, return no explanation.
Otherwise consider every cell once, deleting it when validity survives.
A failed deletion stays failed after subsequent deletions: a smaller valid
set would imply validity of the earlier superset.
Thus the procedure terminates after at most $|D|$ deletion decisions, preserves
validity, and returns an inclusion-minimal answer by Theorem~\ref{thm:min}.
Since $E(a)$, $G(D)$ is true. An explanation therefore exists iff some input
assignment defeats the target; constant-true behavior has none.

A query visits $O(h|Q||\Sigma|)$ recurrence transitions once permitted valuations
are available. Python's current fixing filter adds $O(hm|\Sigma|)$ work; concrete
valuation encoding and certificate adapters have further bit-processing costs.
Finding one answer uses a linear number of validity queries. The recurrence-work
bound is $O(|D|h|Q||\Sigma|)$, not a precise runtime bound for every adapter.
This counts $|\Sigma|=2^m$; it is not a lower bound or a claim for
minimum-cardinality search, all-answer enumeration, or general parity games.
The ordinary decision path retains two state rows per query and caches only
decision bits within a solver invocation. Detailed tables are constructed when
certification or witness extraction requires them. Enumerating every accepted
answer can require exponential output and is an explicit small-instance audit,
not a dependency of ordinary construction or grading. Resource exhaustion is an
incomplete computation, never evidence that the input is mathematically invalid
or has no explanation.

\begin{proposition}[Independent support characterization]
Let $J$ be the inclusion-minimal supports of defeating changes from $a$, and $T$
the inclusion-minimal sets intersecting every member of $J$. The accepted family is
\[
\Min\{t\cup j:t\in T,\ j\in J\}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every defeating support contains a minimal defeating support. Thus $G(C)$ holds
iff $C$ intersects all members of $J$, and $P(C)$ iff $C$ contains some member.
Every valid $C$ contains a minimal hitting set $t$ and a member $j$, whose union
remains valid. Minimality forces equality. Conversely, a valid proper subset
of a minimal such union would contain a smaller such union, a contradiction.
If $J$ is empty, prevention fails everywhere and the family is empty.
\end{proof}
This proof needs no monotonicity of the machine output in its inputs.
The paper proof and exhaustive tests of this characterization are distinguished
from the machine-checked theorem inventory.

\section{Certificates and formal coverage}
A certificate binds the public instance and semantic version to the actual run,
a proposed answer, and the query tables needed for guarantee, prevention and
each deletion. The checker verifies dimensions, domains, every terminal row
and every backward recurrence equation over all machine states.
A true initial query has a permitted defeating input word.
A false query is supported by the complete locally checked table.
One successful path cannot establish a universal guarantee or absence claim.

The checker does not trust the producer's search order or chosen answer.
Minimality follows from checked queries and Theorem~\ref{thm:min}.
A no-answer certificate checks the globally free query.
One canonical answer per compiled item is checked during package construction;
grading still accepts all minimal alternatives by evaluating the submitted
candidate directly. Enumerating and certifying every accepted answer is optional
deep-audit work. Certification is mandatory for publishing a certified package;
retaining detailed certificate records is optional. Verbose console diagnostics
do not alter decisions, and a separate output option saves checked certificates.
A compact manifest binds the package, source, compiler and validation identities.

The Lean development under \texttt{formal/} builds on reviewed Mealy uniqueness
and locality definitions, with vendored-source provenance. New modules separate
combined semantics, terminating deletion, layered recurrence and certificate
checking, finite cell binding, general switch construction, equality reduction
and terminal display projection,
sparse checked-query lookup, and game/parity correspondence. The exact
coverage and executable checks are recorded in \texttt{formal/README.md}
and generated verification artifacts. A theorem count alone is not evidence
that the formal statements match the task.

The semantic construction and terminal projection have the general theorems
above; the SVG renderer does not. A proof that an output-only tree evaluates
correctly does not prove that all successful paths are drawn toward the same
recognizable goal. Canonical cell-key injectivity, insertion of locally checked roots,
lookup binding and required-query coverage are also proved. The certificate
runtime uses these exact lookup and coverage functions, eliminating a redundant
recursive evaluation of the reference oracle after table checking. The remaining
JSON parsing, finite-array assembly, serialization, imperative adapter control
flow, source/digest binding, executable platform and web/session implementation
are explicit tested trust boundaries. Independent per-transition checks validate
every source valuation at every compiled state/round, including the terminal
projection, without enumerating all complete words. Exhaustive small examples, direct quantified oracles,
cross-language checks and deliberate mutations address adapter mismatches.
Neither finite tests nor theorem counts prove human readability. No unrestricted
LTL compiler or policy-solving correctness is claimed.

\section{Dataset, difficulty and release identity}
The standalone constructor produces machines, observed traces and targets before any
game construction. The v5 package recompiles the same source machines, traces,
targets, split assignments and panel membership as v4; its display correction
does not resample the dataset or change accepted explanations. A fixed seed
determines the producer's output; imported datasets use the
same compiler without passing through this producer. The study package preserves
the planned 16 tutorial, 32 pilot, 256 development and 1,024 test roles, with a
separate 64-item matched human--agent panel. Exact released counts, hashes and
executed checks belong to the generated validation block below and the manifest.

The constructor samples explicit total deterministic tables and positive actual
executions. For temporally dependent selection it checks that fixing the final
round alone does not guarantee the target, which implies that every valid
explanation contains an earlier cell. Such filters shape the sampled task
distribution; they do not change the general import contract. Rejection of exact
machine duplicates is not semantic deduplication or evidence of held-out causal
mechanisms. Any stronger split equivalence, leakage control or generalization
claim needs its own declared test and evidence.

State count, input width and trace length define structural sampling strata,
not a validated ordinal difficulty scale. The meaningful quantities also include
reachable alternatives, temporal dependence, irrelevant cells, valid-answer
multiplicity and presentation demands. Adding unreachable states or unused
rounds can enlarge an instance without making its reasoning harder.
TempoBench's structural performance analysis provides hypotheses for calibration,
not thresholds already validated for this interface and answer contract.
Human accuracy, agent accuracy, error type and navigation/time measurements must
establish any empirical scale. The extended agent range need not remain solvable
by humans; conclusions about a human limit require measured human evidence.

Dataset version \texttt{trace-mealy-v5} and protocol version
\texttt{trace-study-v5} retain the normative \texttt{trace.gp.v2} predicate.
The v4 presentation retained separate terminal bulbs for transitions with the

\section{Playable interface and evaluation}
The board exposes the compiled switch routes, outputs and supplied recording.
Each time layer has a mechanism for every state; navigation can focus a region
without changing the semantic graph. Clicking a switch marks its recorded setting
without changing that setting. Repeated appearances of one cell share one mark.
The task is to mark switches that pass two tests: keeping the marked
settings must force the lamp on at the designated target round, and changing
only marked settings must permit it to be off at that round. All successful
terminal routes join the same target lamp; the other exit leaves it dark.
Intermediate output indicators may switch between ON and OFF and are not goals.
No mark may be removable while retaining both properties.
A replay shows the supplied recording without revealing new counterfactuals.

Five untimed animated demonstrations introduce a direct path, conjunction,
disjunction, XOR and a removable extra mark. Demonstrations enumerate all visible
completions for each obligation and distinguish a failed guarantee from failed
prevention. A single successful animation is never presented as proof of a
universal claim. Practice feedback provides the appropriate counterexample or
prevention witness, a certified example answer and redundant-mark feedback.
Where appropriate, the latter compares the recorded setting with a changed
setting to illustrate why a redundant control can lead to the same target.
Such a pair is illustrative, not a complete enumeration or proof of the
universal guarantee; the exact checker establishes the feedback verdict.
Formal instructions, cell IDs and the equivalent transition table are available
in an optional reference rather than required for viewing the puzzle.

\section{Analysis and empirical slots}
The primary comparative statistic is equal-item exact explanation accuracy on
the fixed 64-item panel. Full-pool agent results use 1,024 items.
The registered contrast is agent minus human accuracy. Every accepted minimal
answer earns full credit. Cell overlap against a canonical answer is retired.

Report guarantee failure, prevention failure and valid-but-nonminimal responses
alongside malformed outputs, timeouts, exclusions and withdrawals.
Latency and probes need denominators. Answer shapes do not establish internal
reasoning strategies. There is no scored simulation control in this release,
so it cannot establish a measured SIM/MIN gap.

Humans are participant clusters; an agent run/repetition stays a cluster across
all its forms. Under an outcome-independent fixed observation design, cluster
$c$ has contribution range width
\[
w_c=\sum_{j\text{ observed by }c}\frac{1}{|J|n_j}.
\]
Independence between clusters gives the conservative Hoeffding
radius~\cite{hoeffding1963}
\[
\epsilon_\alpha=\sqrt{\tfrac12\log(2/\alpha)\sum_c w_c^2}.
\]
Combine squared weights for independent population contrasts.
The bound may be wide with few agent cohorts. It does not address informative
missingness, dependent runs, adaptive selection or new benchmark items.
The implemented confirmatory gate requires a pre-collection registration record
binding release, protocol, analysis and model identities, fixed equal human
clusters per form, complete agent run cohorts, and original audit events.
Incomplete or excluded clusters and withdrawals fail this strict design gate.
Registration authenticity and independence are investigator attestations;
software does not prove them. Other designs require separate justification.
Bootstrap summaries are exploratory unless separately justified; hypothetical
calibration does not establish the actual study's assumptions.

Eight judgments per shared-panel item requires 128 usable humans per condition
at four items each. This establishes coverage, not power. Register sample,
stopping and reserve rules after planning and before main outcomes.

\section{Limitations and extension boundary}
Correct labels do not establish human ease, accessibility or construct validity.
Public mechanisms, finite targets, construction filters and presentation choices
constrain interpretation. General compilation can produce a large or visually
complicated puzzle even when every intervention is preserved. Scrolling, zooming
and focused rendering address access, but do not establish intuitive comprehension
or eliminate navigation burden. The v4 target ambiguity demonstrates that
execution and grading checks can pass while a display conveys the wrong goal.
The v5 terminal projection and visual acceptance checks address that defect,
but identifying the goal without technical instructions, comprehension and
enjoyment still require a pilot of the corrected representation.
Visual and serialized presentation remain different modalities.
No human, live-model or infinite-strategy finding follows from software tests.

Removing fixed software caps does not remove computational cost. The explicit
machine alphabet has $2^m$ valuations and the full visual forest scales with both
state count and horizon. All-answer output may itself be exponential. A resource
budget may end a run without deciding it. Each supported input is finite, and the
theorems quantify over arbitrarily large finite inputs; no claim of a completed
infinite computation is made. Neither a small-machine success nor a resource
failure on one workstation determines what larger infrastructure can execute.

A strategy-necessity extension must supply the plant arena, owners, alternative
moves, initial position, baseline policy/memory, editable commitments, background
and effect monitors, and finite candidate language. The reviewed witness theorem
then needs concrete monitor/solver correctness, reachable accepted witnesses,
removal feasibility and exclusion certificates. One favorable lasso does not
prove a universal policy guarantee. Remaining inside a winning region need not
win, and a maximal SCC's minimum can miss losing subcycles. These are extension
gates, not claims of this finite release.
\trialbox{TRIAL-LIMITATIONS}{Update from the actual sample, missingness,
ceiling/floor behavior, model constraints, presentation and unperformed controls.}

\section{Conclusion}
\TRACE{} specifies a finite explanation task with an exact backward-game
interpretation, a general semantics-preserving Mealy-to-switch constructor
and proved terminal display projection,
a terminating minimal-answer algorithm and locally checkable evidence.
Its intended comparison concerns people and AI using a common task, information
and answer contract. Formal correctness and empirical benchmark validity remain
distinct obligations.
\trialbox{TRIAL-CONCLUSION}{Write conclusions from completed evidence, including
null, reversed or ceiling findings. Do not predict a human advantage before measurement.}
