## Standard operator script

Complete the bracketed limits from the assigned session before reading this script. Pilot the script and examples together; do not improvise strategy hints.

“Watch the golden signal. Each switch sends it along one of two wires. Time runs from left to right. Every successful final route reaches the same target lamp. The other exit leaves it dark. Small ON/OFF indicators show earlier outputs; they are not additional goals.

“Click a switch to mark it blue. A mark holds its recorded direction. Your marks must pass two tests: when they are held, the target light is on however the other switches move; when only your marks may change, there is a way to make that target light off. Use no spare marks.

“Watch the shared examples. Identical full labels, including the moment number, are one switch and move together. A at moment 1 and A at moment 2 are different switches. The change test may move some of your marks, not necessarily all of them. Different sets of marks can work.

“You can drag or scroll, zoom, use Overview, or go to a chosen moment and route. The switch strip includes every input at the chosen moment, even if a route does not use it. These controls help you inspect the same puzzle; they do not try a new experiment or stop the clock.

“Practice shows an example answer and what happened, including a route that explains a failed test when available. Scored puzzles allow [ASSIGNED TIME] and one answer, with no correctness feedback. You can change your marks until submission and pause between puzzles. Your assigned condition permits [READ THE ASSIGNED NUMBER: no counterfactual experiments / N counterfactual experiments]. Replaying the recorded signal uses no experiment.

“If no setting of any switches can turn the target light off, open Details & accessibility and check ‘The light cannot be turned off by any switches.’ Leaving every switch unmarked is a different answer.

“You may use blank scratch paper, but no outside code, websites, calculators, AI or help from others. I can explain the controls before scored puzzles, but cannot suggest answers during them.”

## Common examples

These are teaching examples, not hidden test keys.

- Copy: if the target copies one earlier input, selecting that cell both fixes the target and permits prevention by changing it.
- OR with both inputs 1: either single fixed 1 guarantees the target, but neither alone permits prevention. Selecting both meets both rules.
- AND with both inputs 1: changing either alone prevents the target, but one fixed input cannot guarantee it. Select both.
- XOR with inputs (1,0): both recorded values are needed to guarantee output 1. Changing just one of the selected values prevents it; flipping both restores it. This explains why “some change within the group” differs from “flip the whole group.”

Five untimed visual demonstrations show these principles and a removable extra mark. The default two practice puzzles, a solved example for each and personal feedback are available equally in the human examples dialog and fresh agent contexts. The examples do not list every possible correct explanation. Imported packages can supply different practice counts; the investigator must state the actual sequence.

## Comprehension check

1. Do you submit one valid minimal explanation or combine all possible explanations? Expected: one.
2. Does minimal mean fewer cells than every other correct answer? Expected: no; no cell can be removed while keeping both rules.
3. Must a preventing experiment flip every selected cell? Expected: no.
4. If a selection guarantees the target but cannot prevent it, is it sufficient? Expected: no.
5. Can a scored answer be replaced after submission? Expected: no.
6. How many experiments are allowed in this condition? Expected: the assigned number, zero in reasoning.
7. If an earlier light is on but the target light is off at its moment, did the target happen? Expected: no.
8. Do repeated copies of one switch label represent independently changeable switches? Expected: no.
9. If two final routes reach the same target lamp, does taking the other route by itself prevent the target? Expected: no.
10. Can a switch be unnecessary after an earlier switch is held, yet matter on another route? Expected: yes.
11. Does zooming or looking at another moment reset the timer or consume an experiment? Expected: no.

Use the same explanation when clarifying. Record misunderstanding and revisions in the pilot. Do not exclude participants based on practice accuracy.

## Scored trials and debrief

Offer operational reminders only. Do not reveal correctness, answer cells or causal hints. Record technical failures; do not silently grant extra time. Leaving before or between items ends the session without creating timeouts for unstarted items.

At debrief ask about unclear rules, difficult displays, linked switches, target timing, navigation, enjoyment, technical problems and time burden. Record answers only under the actual data policy. Explain that population comparisons concern aggregate task performance and that human ease or superiority has not been assumed. The visual and structured AI presentations share task information; equal interface effort has not been assumed. Provide the completed contact and withdrawal details.
